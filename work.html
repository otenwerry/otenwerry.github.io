Why?

It seems likely that we’ll develop AIs that are better than the best humans in nearly every domain within my lifetime, maybe much sooner. I think this poses a number of serious, unprecedented, civilization-scale threats. For example, we currently seem to not be very good at robustly instilling good values in LLMs. If we don’t get better at this soon, we may end up with superintelligent models whose values look alien to us; these misaligned models may try to escape human control, and they may succeed. If we manage to solve this problem, we may still end up with extremely powerful tools that massively uplift how much bad actors can accomplish, such as by helping a malicious group engineer a pandemic. And on top of all this, sufficiently general-purpose AI may make it very difficult for many people to find employment. This could lead to extreme concentration of power in the hands of a few AI developers while the average person's main bargaining chip, their labor, becomes nearly worthless.

Nevertheless, there are a lot of future possibilities to be excited about: AGI could revolutionize medicine, create immense abundance, give people way more free time, etc. But we have to safely navigate the risks above in order to fully realize these possibilities, and right now I don’t think that we’re on track.

In short, I think we may be in the early stages of one of the most pivotal moments of human history, and I’m trying to do what I can to help figure out how to make things go well.
